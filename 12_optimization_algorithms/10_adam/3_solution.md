- The reason we want to reduce the learning rate as we converge is linked to the fact that we do SGD/minibatchSGD. In each step we only get a noisy estimate of the gradient at that location. Initially it is fine because the noise is not much compared to our moving direction and we roughly move towards our target. However, as we close-in on the optimum, the noise makes it difficult to close-in well enough on the optimum. Without adjusting the learning rate near the optimum, we will just bounce around it and never converge fully. Hence, we like to reduce learning rate with time (for each coordinate differently, according to how much data from that coordinate has been assesed, which is effective time elapsed for that coordinate) so that the effect of noise in gradient due to random sampling is reduced with time. The s (scale) term allows us to do this in Adam, Adagrad and RMSProp.
