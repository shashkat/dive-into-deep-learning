- Probably an example of such a scenario would be when due to some reason (either due to specific sample of data in SGD, or due to strange optimization function) the gradient was relatively small for past few iterations, but suddenly for one step it is huge. This could lead to s term getting super high suddenly (effectily forgetting about past, small gradients), and slow convergence. Since the s term has been high once, it will tend to remain high for more timepoints in the future, worsening the effect of slow convergence. Hence, a larger minibatch can be useful to solve this as it will remove such strange/abrupt effects due to bad sample of data.
